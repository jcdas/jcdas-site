What I bring3 strengths, with receipts

Where I am different from the next account executive.

01

Deal architect, not a pitcher.

Most account executives fight over the percentage. I win on the structure of the agreement: who pays what, when, against which commitment. Price is the last thing we talk about.

Receipts
  • A credit on subscription fees already paid, approved by HQ, turned a defensive renewal into a 5-year early renewal with upsell.
  • A sandbox billed only from year 2, because the customer would not have time to use it in year 1. The line that let a pharma group say yes to a 5-year term.
  • A per-plant base rate with 4 conditional discounts: firm term, reference customer, single platform. The customer pays less, I get a reference and exclusivity. A trade, not a discount.
02

I read German buying committees from the inside.

14 years in delivery and consulting before I carried a quota. I know which 2 or 3 people in a Konzern have to say yes before procurement opens the file, what each of them needs to be true, and I write to them in German.

Receipts
  • First email as the new account executive of an industrial group: not a pitch, a heads-up on a purchase option expiring at year end. Useful for them, a meeting for me.
  • A trip to a group's US business unit argued to my own management as the path to a much larger sister entity at its ERP migration. A position on the board, not a visit.
  • An indexation dispute with a global tire manufacturer settled on the customer's own comparison method, 4,07%, relationship intact.
03

Orchestrator, without authority.

The largest FY25 deal needed 20+ colleagues across services, legal, pricing, presales and partners aligned on one deadline. None of them report to me. It closed in Q4.

Receipts
  • The note after signature named every contributor, by team. Colleagues and customers get the same version of me.
  • When I misread a question from the deal desk, the next email said so in its first line, then fixed it. Speed of correction beats being right.

How I sell3 deals, anonymised

Told the way they actually happened.

Names and exact figures are withheld, my customers did not sign up to be on a website. The mechanics are real.

01
Sensor and automation group · Q4 2025

From eroded trust to an early 5-year renewal

Situation
A long-standing customer whose trust in the platform had eroded, well before the end of term. The safe play was to wait and defend the renewal.
The move
Stopped selling. Brought value engineering and services in for a joint blueprint of what the platform should deliver for them, in their numbers. Then orchestrated 20+ colleagues across services, legal, pricing and presales around one deadline.
EUR 8MTCV, 5-year SaaS agreement, high 6-figure ACV upsell, signed early
02
Pharma and chemicals group · mid 2025

Legacy on-premise estate to the cloud, without a price war

Situation
Planning software running on-premise for years. The customer wanted the cloud, procurement wanted the on-premise price, HQ wanted a 5-year term.
The move
Split the problem. A ramped subscription over 5 years, a sandbox the customer only pays for from year 2 when they can use it, services rates fixed for the term. Every party got the line it needed to say yes.
5 yrsSaaS term at about EUR 1M a year, signed in June
03
Building materials group · 2024 to 2026

From a paid blueprint to a platform, one signature at a time

Situation
A family-owned group with plants on 3 continents, several planning tools, and a strong preference for proof before commitment.
The move
Sold a paid blueprint first, small enough to sign fast. Delivered it. Used the result to sign the cloud subscription agreement, then a planning transformation SOW, then a change request. Opened the US business unit at CFO, CCO and COO level on the back of it.
4signatures in 24 months, each one funding the next conversation
What these have in common: I do not pitch. I find the 2 or 3 people in a German Konzern who actually have to say yes, I learn what each of them needs to be true, and I build the deal around that. MEDDIC is the checklist, 14 years of delivery is the reason they let me in.
